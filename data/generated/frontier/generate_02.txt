Distance learning, in which students attend classes online or through video conferencing, is now offered by a growing number of schools. Although it is sometimes presented as the future of education, I believe that students benefit more from attending school in person. Physical classrooms support mental health, provide practical learning opportunities, and create a sense of community that online classes cannot match.

The mental health of young people is closely tied to their connections with others. School provides daily contact with friends, teachers, and mentors, and these relationships offer support during difficult times. When students learn from home, many of these connections fade. Experiences during periods of widespread remote learning showed that many students felt lonely, anxious, and disconnected. While online classes can deliver information, they cannot easily provide the sense of belonging that comes from being part of a school community.

In-person schooling also offers practical learning experiences that are difficult to reproduce online. Science experiments, art projects, music ensembles, physical education, and technical subjects all require equipment, space, and hands-on guidance. A student cannot learn to use laboratory equipment safely or play in an orchestra through a screen. Even in academic subjects, face-to-face discussion and collaborative problem-solving help students think critically and learn from different perspectives.

Moreover, schools are communities that teach values and responsibilities beyond the curriculum. Students learn to respect rules, resolve conflicts, and contribute to something larger than themselves, whether through student government, sports teams, or volunteer projects. These experiences help shape responsible citizens. When students spend their school years at home, they miss many of the opportunities that help them grow into confident, capable adults.

Supporters of distance learning argue that it offers flexibility and can help students who face obstacles to attending school. This is true, and for some students, online learning can be an important alternative. However, the needs of a minority should be addressed through targeted programs rather than by encouraging all students to learn from home.

In conclusion, while distance learning has a place in modern education, it should not replace the traditional classroom for most students. Attending school in person supports students' well-being, provides valuable practical experience, and builds the sense of community that is essential to a complete education.
